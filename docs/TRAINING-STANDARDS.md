# Training standards

The rules every generated session must meet. `scripts/check-programs.mjs` enforces the
measurable ones on every build; a change that breaks one cannot be published.

These were written without a certified coach. Treat them as a starting point and have a
qualified trainer review them, and the programs, before public launch.

## Session structure

1. Every full session has, in order: warm-up, a Tatva drill or skill practice, the main
   workout, at least one accessory block when time allows, and a cool-down.
2. Warm-up is at least 3 moves; cool-down at least 3 (2 when the athlete shortens the day).
3. The main workout has at least 3 different moves.

## Session length

| Athlete's chosen length | A full session must run |
|---|---|
| 30 min | 20–34 min |
| 45 min | 32–49 min |
| 60 min | 39–65 min, and at least half of sessions 50 min or more |

Shortened days: 15-minute express runs 13–17 min; 20-minute runs at most 26 min.
Suggested length at onboarding: beginner 30, some experience 45, regular 60.

## Safety

1. Quiet mode: no jumping or impact moves anywhere in the session.
2. Small space: no move that needs more room than a mat.
3. Sore or limited body part: no move that loads that part.
4. No single set asks for more than 60 reps or a hold longer than 90 seconds.
5. A journey's first and last session is the same benchmark, so progress is measurable.

## Progression

1. Volume changes by at most 30% from one session to the next, and stays within 0.5–2.0×.
2. There are six skill ladders (push, shoulders, legs, hips & back, core, engine). Every rung
   has a goal: a clean amount per set above the move's starting amount. A ladder moves up
   after two workouts rated strict form and effort 3/5 or less in which its current move
   reached the goal in a set. Sets below the goal or with slipping form never count.
   Broken form, an exhausting (5/5) session, or marking the move "too hard" moves the athlete
   down one rung.
3. Each journey has a deload week before its final week.

## Signature content

1. A Peak's recipe never changes. Splits (Base Camp, Ridge, Summit) only change the number
   of rounds, and each split keeps its own personal best.
2. Only the Peak itself is timed, not the warm-up, drill, accessory work or cool-down.
